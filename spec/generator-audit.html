Why the same puzzle kept coming back

Two complaints — “very many puzzles with the same patterns”, and “−, ÷ and [] are used very seldom” — turned out to be two unrelated faults, one in the draw and one in the target band. Both are fixed. This is what was measured, what was decided, and what changed.

The headline

What changed

Two cells matter most: 3 Zahlen, alle vier is the documented default selection and where the complaint actually lived, and 4 Zahlen, alle vier, groß was the worst case in the whole search space.

The diagnosis

Same symptom, opposite causes

Every figure was measured twice: over 200 real draws, and exhaustively over every (numbers, target) pair the band admits. Where the draw matched the pool, the band was at fault. Where the draw was far narrower, the draw was throwing variety away.

Fault 1 · the draw

At 3 and 4 numbers, variety existed and was discarded

reachable() returns every target the drawn numbers can hit, and nextPuzzle kept the first one at the best rank — a strict > against a running best, so ties fell to enumeration order. That order is fixed: flat arrangements before bracketed ones, operator tuples in + − × ÷. Brackets lost every tie by construction.

Fixed twice over: the draw collects the tied candidates and picks at random, and history.ts now keeps a second window of recently-seen shapes so the draw can prefer one it has not just shown.

Fault 2 · the band

At 2 numbers, the band had already chosen the operator

With two single digits a − b ≤ 8 and a ÷ b ≤ 9, while the großes Ziel band began at 12. The band started above everything − and ÷ can reach, so the pool itself held none — the draw was reporting an empty shelf faithfully, and no draw fix could have touched it.

Fixed by giving those selections one band instead of three. Searching every legal boundary placement confirmed no three-way split works.

Decided by the product owner

Three changes

A floor, not a ceiling

The old preference demanded as many distinct operators as a draw could offer — added so four operators would not feel like one, and the single biggest cause of the monotony: three distinct operators from four numbers admits 3 of the 25 shapes the pool holds.

It asks for two now. Carved out where two is impossible: two numbers have one operator position, and {+,−} always keeps a whole-number one-operator route through a−(b−c) = a−b+c.

{×,÷} is not carved out, and that is a change of fact rather than policy — its identity a÷(b÷c) = a·c÷b runs through a fraction, so once fractional routes stopped counting, 14 % of three-number and 48 % of four-number ×÷ puzzles genuinely need both.

Whole numbers, or no puzzle

The evaluator only ever checked the final result, which made this a legal puzzle:

9 ÷ (1 ÷ 9 ÷ 9) = 729

The bracket is 1⁄81, and dividing by it multiplies. Where ÷ is selected without ×, that is the only route to a large target — so it was 79 % of 4 Zahlen, +÷, groß, not an edge case. Refused outright now, on the decision that this is a correctness question for a first-grade audience rather than a taste one.

Two numbers was 0 % everywhere, so the youngest players were never exposed. The weaker cousin — a chip that changes nothing, × 1 or 6 ÷ 6 — stayed a preference, and measures 0–3 % of draws.

One to three bands

A band is a slice of target magnitude, and magnitude is a proxy for operator. Rather than always three, a selection now carries as many as keep every selected operator available, on boundaries a player can read off a chip.

28 of the 33 reachable selections keep three. Three get two; 2 Zahlen with three or four operators gets one, labelled beliebig rather than a “klein” that covers the whole range.

Regenerating the table over the whole-number pool also emptied bands that were entirely fractional, so they had to be re-derived rather than re-filtered.

Fault 2, in full

The band table, then and now

Boundaries are round numbers now, the overlap bug is gone — the old generator sliced the sorted target list by index, so a value sitting on a tertile boundary landed in both neighbouring bands — and five selections carry fewer than three.
